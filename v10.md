# v0.10 — plan

What `ROADMAP.md` v0.10 asks for, in the order the dependencies actually run,
with the decision behind each piece written down before it is built rather than
after. The shape of this document is `docs/v07v08.md`'s: a section per group, the
reasoning first and the file list second, because the file list is the part that
is obvious once the reasoning is settled.

The roadmap lists eighteen bullets and they are not eighteen independent pieces
of work. Three things gate most of the rest:

- **`Attachment` is the fence post.** Beams, trails and particle emitters are all
  parented to one in the vocabulary this engine is copying, so a version of any
  of them built before it exists is a version that gets rebuilt.
- **The sequences are already here.** `core::NumberSequence` and
  `core::ColorSequence` have existed since v0.6 with a fixed twenty-keypoint
  capacity, an `Envelope` field nothing reads, and Luau bindings. What v0.10 adds
  is the *keypoint* constructors, the `ecs::PropertyType` cases that let one be a
  property, and the first consumer — which is the particle emitter, and which is
  what the `Envelope` field was put there for.
- **The particle system decides the render architecture, not the other way
  round.** 100,000 emitters at five particles each is half a million particles a
  frame. That number is what rules out every arrangement where a particle is an
  entity, a `DrawInstance`, or anything with a `core::Name` in it.

---

## 1. The scale constraint, settled first

**A particle is not an entity and must never become one.** At half a million
particles an `ecs` row per particle is half a million archetype rows churning
every frame — create and destroy are the two operations an archetype store is
worst at, and a particle's whole life is one of each. `physics/AGENTS.md` already
makes the neighbouring argument for why a sleeping body moves archetype rather
than carrying a flag; this is the same reasoning pointed the other way. Particles
are pooled in a resource and the *emitter* is the entity.

**A particle is not a `scene::DrawInstance`.** That type is 80 bytes and carries
two `core::Name`s, a full `CFrame` and an `AlphaMode`. Half a million of them is
40 MB written per frame, and none of the fields is one a particle varies: every
particle from one emitter shares a texture, a blend mode and an orientation rule.
So the emitter carries what is shared and the particle carries what is not.

`ParticleInstance` is **32 bytes**, and each field is there because it varies per
particle within one emitter:

| field | bytes | why per-particle |
|---|---|---|
| `Position` | 12 | it moved |
| `Size` | 4 | `Size` sequence over its own age |
| `Rotation` | 4 | `RotSpeed` integrated over its own age |
| `Colour` | 4 (RGBA8) | `Color` and `Transparency` sequences over its own age |
| `Frame` | 2 | flipbook cell, from its own age |
| `EmitterSlot` | 2 | which emitter's texture and blend state to draw with |
| `Reserved` | 4 | named, for the reason every `Reserved` in the engine is named |

28 bytes × 500,000 = 16 MB a frame, and the emitter slot is what keeps the shared
half out of it. That is the whole design in one table.

**Storage is a slab of pooled arrays, not a `std::vector` per emitter.** 100,000
vectors is 100,000 allocations and 100,000 pointer chases to reach one particle.
The pool is one array of `ParticleState` (the simulation half — velocity, age,
lifetime, seed) and one array of `ParticleInstance` (the presentation half),
indexed the same way, with each emitter owning a contiguous **capacity block**
assigned when it starts emitting. A block is `Rate × Lifetime` rounded up and
capped, so an emitter's particles are contiguous and the step loop is a linear
walk that `parallel::Jobs` can slice by block.

**Dead particles are swapped to the end of their own block, not compacted
globally.** A global compaction is a data dependency across every worker; a swap
inside a block touches only that block's memory and keeps the live prefix
contiguous for the upload.

**The emitter's own randomness is per-emitter, never global.** `core::Random` is
seeded from the emitter's entity id and the world's tick, so two runs of one scene
emit the same particles — the determinism rule, applied to the one system in the
engine whose whole job is to look random. `NumberKeypoint::Envelope` is sampled
against that generator, which is exactly what `Sequence.hpp` says it declined to
do itself and why.

## 2. The order of work

Each group builds on the one above it. Nothing below is startable before the
thing above it exists, which is why the roadmap's own order is not the order here.

### Group A — the vocabulary the rest needs

1. **`core::Vector3::One`**, and `zero`/`one` on the Luau and TypeScript
   `Vector3`. `Vector3::Zero` already exists in C++; the script side has neither.
2. **`NumberSequenceKeypoint` and `ColorSequenceKeypoint` constructors** in both
   bindings. The types exist and the keypoints are unreachable from a script,
   which makes a twenty-stop gradient unauthorable — the same gap
   `ecs::PropertyType`'s v0.8 comment describes for `UDim2`.
3. **`ecs::PropertyType::NumberSequence` / `ColorSequence` / `NumberRange`**.
   `PropertyType` is a closed list on purpose and growing it is a visible change:
   three cases in `game::Values`, three widgets in the properties panel, three in
   each binding. A particle emitter has eight sequence properties and none of
   them can be a property until this lands.
4. **`Enum.Material`**, replacing `Enum_Material` in the generated bindings. A
   nested table rather than a flattened name, which is what a Roblox script
   spells. The underlying `ecs::EnumTable` is unchanged — this is a change to the
   *generator*, `mono.tools/bindings`, and to nothing else.

### Group B — `Attachment` and `Attributes`

5. **`scene::Attachment`** — a `CFrame` relative to a parent `BasePart`, plus the
   resolved world frame. Roblox's semantics: `CFrame` is the local offset,
   `WorldCFrame` is derived. **Derived once a frame by a system, not per read**,
   because a beam reads both of its attachments' world frames every frame and a
   getter that walked to the parent would be two tree walks per beam per frame.

   This is the one place the "every transform is world space and nothing
   propagates it" rule in `scene/Components.hpp` gets an exception, and it is
   worth stating why it is not one: an `Attachment` is not a `PVInstance` and
   carries no `Transform`. It carries its *own* component with two frames in it,
   and a single system resolves the second from the first. There is still no
   transform hierarchy and no dirty cascade — there is one flat pass over one
   component type.

6. **`ecs::Attributes`** — `GetAttribute`/`SetAttribute` on every `Instance`.
   The storage question is the whole of this item. An attribute is an arbitrary
   name to an arbitrary value, which is exactly the shape a component column
   cannot hold — so it is a **resource**, an `AttributeTable` keyed by
   `(Entity, Name)`, not a component. That keeps `Instance`'s component set the
   size it is (an attribute table on every row would be a map per row) and keeps
   the value union out of the archetype.

   `AttributeChanged` fires through the existing `Signals` path.

### Group C — lights, beams, trails

7. **`scene::Light`** — one component with a `LightKind` (`Point`, `Spot`,
   `Surface`), because the three differ by two fields and a class each. Three
   components would be three columns, three queries and three upload paths for
   what the renderer packs into one array either way. The classes are three;
   the storage is one, which is the same trade `Collider::Extent` makes across
   three shapes.

8. **`effects::Beam` and `effects::Trail`** — both are ribbons between two
   points, and that is not a coincidence worth ignoring: a beam is a ribbon
   between two attachments with a Bezier curve through it, and a trail is a
   ribbon between two attachments *sampled over time*. One ribbon builder, two
   sources of points. The generated geometry is a `RibbonVertex` stream the
   renderer uploads, which means neither needs a mesh and neither is a
   `DrawInstance`.

### Group D — the particle system (the bulk)

9. **`mono.engine/effects`**, a new module at **L8 · shared**, beside `physics`
   and above `scene`. Shared rather than client because a server simulates
   particles for anything gameplay reads off them and because a headless test is
   the only way this gets checked at all — the same argument
   `scene::OrderScene` makes for living in `shared` rather than in `render`.

   Contents: `ParticleEmitter` (the component, the full Roblox property
   surface), `ParticleSystem` (the pool and the step), `ParticleInstance` (the
   32-byte payload), `Ribbon` (beams and trails), and `Registration`.

10. **The renderer's particle pass.** A billboard quad expanded in the vertex
    shader from `gl_VertexIndex` — no vertex buffer at all for the quad, because
    four corners of a unit square are two bit tests and a fetch is a fetch. One
    instance buffer of `ParticleInstance`, sorted back to front per view when the
    emitter blends and unsorted when it adds (additive blending is
    order-independent, which is why every particle system in the world offers it).

11. **The stress scene**, `Particles.luau`, and `engine.effects.bench.particles`.
    The number in the roadmap is the acceptance test: 100,000 emitters, five
    particles each, and a figure in a comment for what it actually costs.

### Group E — camera, character, input

12. **`CurrentCamera`** on `Workspace`, a `Reference` property over the
    `ActiveCamera` resource. Reading it resolves the resource; writing it moves
    the resource's entity. That makes `workspace.CurrentCamera.CFrame = ...` work
    without a second place the live camera is recorded.
13. **`scene::CameraController`** — the resource holding camera mode
    (`Classic`, `LockFirstPerson`, `Scriptable`), zoom distance and its clamps.
    A resource because there is one live camera. The *arithmetic* is in `scene`
    (shared, testable); the *events that drive it* come from `input` (client).
14. **`scene::InputState`** — a shared resource holding the current keyboard,
    mouse and gamepad state, written by the client from SDL, read by scripts.
    This is the seam `UserInputService` needs: `input` is L12 client and `script`
    is L9 shared, so the state cannot pass through the module that produces it.
15. **`UserInputService` and `ContextActionService`** as script globals over that
    resource, with `ContextActionService` owning a priority stack of bound
    actions — which is the whole of what it adds over the former.
16. **`scene::Humanoid`** and a character controller: a capsule, a move
    direction, a jump, and ground detection through `physics::Raycast`. Kept
    deliberately small — the roadmap says "basic", and the user's instruction says
    to spend the time on the particles instead.

### Group F — the CDN folder and the assets manager

17. **`~/Documents/atomic-game-engine/cdn/{raw,processed}`**, flat, hash-named,
    with a log file. `cdn::LocalStore` becomes the default source for the cdn
    service, the client and `unified_server_client`, which is what "simplify
    tests" means in practice: a test stops needing a server to fetch from.
18. **The upload button and the uploaded-asset browser**, in `mono.studio`.
19. **Seed the folder** with the files the roadmap lists, and **GIF support** —
    a decoder in `bake` producing a flipbook texture, which is the same shape the
    particle flipbook already needs and is why the two are in one version.

## 4. What is deliberately not in v0.10

- **A GPU particle simulation.** The step is a CPU parallel loop and the upload
  is per frame. A compute-shader step is the right answer at ten million and the
  wrong answer at half a million, because at this size the upload dominates and a
  compute path buys a second copy of every emitter's rules living in a shader.
- **Particle collision.** Roblox has it; it needs a broad-phase query per
  particle and that is a different order of cost. The property is not declared,
  rather than declared and ignored — `SurfaceAppearance`'s rule about
  `MetalnessMap`.
- **The node editors** in the v0.11 bullet. They are v0.11 and the temptation to
  start them early is exactly what that bullet's placement is for.

## 5. What actually landed, against this plan

Written after the work rather than before it, because the plan was a plan and
this is the record. `ROADMAP.md` v0.10 carries the per-item reasoning; this is the
shape.

**Done and verified** — eighteen of v0.10's twenty items. 196 test suites pass,
both languages typecheck, the architecture graph and the bindings drift check
agree, `just format-check` is clean, and `just determinism` and
`just replay-check` are byte-identical.

**One item is `[~]`**: the assets manager's upload is a typed path rather than a
file dialog, because there is no portable picker in this stack. The panel
compiles and links and nobody has opened it.

**`Enum.Material` was `[~]` and is not.** The first pass concluded Luau could not
express a dotted type, probed a definitions file, got "Unknown type", and filed
it as impossible. The probe was right and the conclusion did not follow: a
definitions *file* cannot declare one, and the **host** can register one — which
is exactly what Roblox does. `scriptcheck` now aliases every `Enum_*` under the
`Enum` prefix and `local m: Enum.Material` typechecks. `DEFERRED.md` D00028
carries the correction rather than being deleted.

**What was left out of an otherwise-complete item, stated where it happens**: the
"live renderer" clause of the CDN line, which is an asset preview and wants the
studio viewport to render something that is not a world.

### What the plan got wrong

Worth keeping, because the plan was written confidently and three of its numbers
were not right:

- **`ParticleInstance` is 28 bytes, not 32.** The table in §1 was arithmetic done
  by hand and the compiler disagreed. 28 is better and the `static_assert` now
  pins it.
- **The plan said the sequences' `Envelope` field was "what it was put there
  for".** It is sampled at spawn for `Lifetime`, `Speed`, `Rotation` and
  `RotSpeed` — but those are `NumberRange`s, not sequences, so `Envelope` still
  has no consumer. The field remains unread.
- **§1 said the emitter's randomness would be `core::Random`.** It is not:
  `core::Random::Float` is a SHA-256 per number, and a scene emitting twenty
  thousand particles a second would run two hundred thousand SHA-256 blocks a
  second to decide where smoke goes. What was kept is the property that mattered —
  a pure function of `(emitter, spawn index, purpose)`, so a recording replays.

### What only running it found

The plan reasoned carefully about the simulation and said nothing about the
draw, and every one of the four real bugs was on the other side of that line:

1. The render pass issued **one draw call per emitter** — 1,608 on a
   1,600-emitter scene, and would have been 100,000 at the target.
2. The upload memcpy'd into the transfer buffer and **never issued the copy** to
   the vertex buffer. It drew the previous frame's contents, so it took a
   screenshot rather than a crash.
3. The refresh pass re-sampled four curves per emitter per frame and was **70 per
   cent of the frame** — 6.4 million `Evaluate` calls for tables that do not
   change.
4. The stress scene's own host parts were `Transparency = 1`, which *submits*
   them into the sorted blended pass: a hundred thousand invisible cubes sorted
   back-to-front every frame, at 4 FPS.

Three of those contradicted a comment that had argued the other way, and the
comments have been corrected in place.
